Abstract—In the online advertising business, it is important for an advertisement with a specific bid to forecast the number of impressions. Given the reliably predicted impressions, advertisers can set their bids reasonably to guarantee the return on investment. Ad impression forecasting also plays a role in various services, such as bid simulator, high-value advertising screening, and programmatic advertising bid adjustment. Although several impression forecasting tools are already widely used in the industry, previous work is pageview-level methods, which have to calculate the winning score for each impression opportunity and are relatively time-consuming. Besides, due to the data flow of the advertising system is too large, sampling storage leads to low advertising coverage and inaccurate estimation of winning rate. To address these issues, in this work, we propose a novel ad-level combined method for impression forecasting framework, which combines XGBoost and gaussian distribution modeling based on RANSAC. In particular, XGBoost improves the model's generalization ability for the missing feature values of ads, and gaussian distribution modeling solves the problems of outlier and sparse sampling while ensuring monotonicity. We deployed the proposed framework in the Tencent online display advertising system and evaluated the effectiveness and efficiency of the framework on a real-world dataset. The experimental results show our design outperforms the existing methods in the accuracy of ad impression forecasting. Meanwhile, our framework achieves 100% ad coverage rate and significantly reduces the average execution time for online serving.

Index Terms—Online Advertising, Ad Impression Forecasting, RTB Advertising

# I. INTRODUCTION

Ad impression forecasting is an important service to estimate the impressions for a given ad with different bids, which helps advertisers avoid blind bidding and achieve their return over investment (ROI). Real-time bidding (RTB), aka programmatic buying, has recently become an important business model for online display advertising in the age of big data. Based on the analysis of massive amounts of cookie data generated by Internet users, RTB has transformed the traditional “media-buying” and “ad-slot buying” patterns to the big-data-driven “audience buying” pattern [1]. There are four main parties in a typical RTB system: Internet user, publisher, advertiser, and ad platform. In a typical RTB system, each time a user opens a web page (or app page) of a publisher, one or multiple pageviews (also called impressions) are generated. Each pageview triggers an ad impression auction. 1) the publisher will send the user information to the ad platform; 2) based on characterizing the user data, the ad platform will find the matched advertisers according to their advertising target rules; 3) after that, the matched advertisers will be informed to submit bids for the opportunity of displaying ads to the user; 4) the winner will be charged at a cost according to the auction rule, and this cost eventually becomes the revenue of the publisher and the ad platform according to some agreed contract. Typically, the advertiser with the highest eCPM (effective cost per mille) score wins the ad impression opportunity. For example, the eCPM score is calculated by multiplying the provided bid value and the estimated pCTR and pCVR for the charging method of CPA. pCTR is the click probability and pCVR is the conversion probability. Recently, with some more sophisticated ad diversity considerations, e.g., user freshness and page diversity, more complicated ad sorting and filtering rules have been proposed [2], [3].

Generally speaking, there are three main considerations in the ad displaying system. Firstly, for the users, the quality (e.g., semantic relevance) of the delivered ads is crucial to ensure their long-term and deep engagement (e.g., click, conversion) in the ad system. Secondly, the abundance of displayed ads on pages is essential for the guarantee of the publishers' revenue. Last but not least, the advertisers need well-designed bidding strategies to achieve their advertising objective (e.g., advertisers may want to maximize the number of impressions/clicks/conversions for a given budget). To balance the interests among users, publishers and advertisers, present display advertising systems usually adopt a three-layer funnel-shaped structure to screen and sort hundreds of ads from billions of ad candidates. In addition, the ad platform also provides various methods of charging advertisers for users' actions, such as CPM, CPC, CPA, and oCPA.

As shown in Figure 1, the three-layer funnel-shaped structure is popular in many companies [2]. When a user visits platform and the system get impression opportunities(a pageview and an impression opportunity are equivalent in this paper.), the first matching layer will find all ads whose target rules match the attributes (e.g., age and gender) of the user from the candidate ads pool with tens of millions of ads. Then, the ranking layer calculates the eCPM score of each ad candidate and after sorting by score, the top hundreds of ad candidates enter into the reranking layer. The reranking layer recalculates the eCPM score, then the ad with the highest score who survive the system filtering rules (e.g., frequency) will be exposed. Note that both the ranking layer and the reranking layer calculate the eCPM score in the same way for ranking. The difference lies in that the ranking layer uses a lightweight CTR/CVR prediction model (e.g., [4]) to do a rough ranking while the reranking layer applies some more sophisticated and time-consuming model (e.g..Wide & Deep [5], DeepFM [6], DeepIFM [7]) to do a fine-grained ranking.

Under such a complicated ad delivery system, it is hard for advertisers to design well-behaved bidding strategies to achieve their advertising goals. To help the advertisers assess the impact of the bid value adjustment on the number of ad impressions, some industry tools have been designed by companies such as Google [8] and Facebook. [9]. However, the problem has remained relatively unexplored in literature. [10] has firstly proposed an impression forecasting method for contextual advertising based on replaying past impression data. That is, This kind of past replay method check whether the ad could be shown when it was present in the system with the bid. Clearly, it ignores the future fluctuations of impression opportunities. [11] then developed an impression forecasting system for sponsored search. For a given advertisement, they use Bayes net trained for the keyword to generate auctions and then assess win or loss for the ad in each auction to determine the total number of impressions. Note that the total future number of auctions of each keyword is estimated by Dynamic Linear Model (DLM). However, for the display advertising system, the number of impression opportunities retrieved by target rules of the given advertisement may reach the order of hundreds of millions. Their approaches are quite resource-consuming because they have to judge whether each impression opportunity can win. Moreover, due to the data flow of the advertising system is too large, the data of impression opportunities with auction information(e.g., bid, eCPM score, winning score) in the reranking layer is sampled during storage. [10] can't serve all the ads because of incomplete sampling data with missing values. Besides, the problems of outlier and sparse sampling(in §III-A) lead to inaccurate estimation of winning rate. Therefore, pageview-level methods like [10] and [11] are not directly applicable to the current advertising system.

To tackle the aforementioned challenges, we propose a novel Ad-level Combined Method for Impression Forecasting ACMIF algorithm. When given an advertisement ad and its bid b, our method first estimates the benchmark impressions and adjustment coefficient separately. By multiplying the benchmark impressions and adjustment coefficient, we obtain the total number of impressions. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

- We propose a novel ad impression forecasting algorithm ACMIF combining XGBoost and gaussian distribution modeling based on RANSAC. We aim to solve the problems of outlier and sparse sampling in pageview-level methods. It also avoids the problem of excessive resource consumption due to the calculation of winning eCPM score for each pageview in the traditional methods.   
- We build a system that uses ACMIF to provide complete services. Our system is more engineeringly lightweight.   
- ACMIF has been deployed in the Tencent online display advertising system. To compare with other methods, we evaluate our framework on a real-world dataset. The experimental results show our design outperforms the existing methods and can significantly increase the ad impression forecasting accuracy. On the Dataset #1, our method provides a approximately 7%(19%) and 3%(10%) relative improvement over THR [10] and GMIFL [11] when $\tau = 0.2(0.5)$ . Furthermore, our framework achieves 100% coverage rate with minimal average execution time for online serving.

# II. RELATED WORK

# A. Ad Impression Forecasting

For ad impression forecasting, existing works perform a two-step process:

- generates sample auctions in which the given ad has an opportunity to appear.   
- counts the number of wins for the ad by comparing the eCPM score of the given ad against the winning eCPM score in the auction.

Wang et al. [10] proposed a search-based method that executes a two-step process to estimate the number of impressions for a given contextual ad. When adapted to the display advertising system, it essentially replays the past auctions with the winning score, which does not consider that the future auctions are fluctuant due to various reasons such as vacation and holiday. We call it Training Hour Replay (THR) which extends from [11]. Cui et al. [12] combine a star tree and a mixture-of-log-normal model to predict bid distribution, which is regarded as the foundation of ad impression forecasting and also neglect fluctuations of the number of future auction. It does not fit our system with various methods of charging. The reason is that, in an auction, the winning score is fixed, but the winning bid is different caused by the different charging methods.

![](images/618c4a253616b76629ac6601b81906dd224b3023ea085b129c9820f18fb84fdd.jpg)



Fig. 2. Feature for estimation of Benchmark Impressions.

Nath et al. proposed GMIF [11] which is an ad impression forecasting method proposed for sponsored search. They build Bayes net per each keyword to model the auctions. To predict the number of auctions, it uses Dynamic Linear Model (DLM) to estimate the number of searches for a keyword and calculate the participation ratio of the given ad under target rules. However, display ads lack search keywords and the existence of tens of thousands of attribute combinations makes it difficult to build Bayes net for predicting the winning score of auction. In addition, the number of impression opportunities retrieved from the database according to the target rules of the given advertisement may reach the order of hundreds of millions. In this case, calculating the eCPM score for each impression opportunity will be very time-consuming. Besides, it is also quite difficult to store the detailed information of all candidates for each pageview, and modern advertising systems(e.g.. Tencent online display advertising system.) often adopt sampling methods. Incomplete data not only makes the system unable to provide services for all advertisements, but also causes the inaccurate estimation of winning rate due to the problems of outlier and sparse sampling(in §III-A).

Note that the "ad impression forecasting" in [13] refers to the problem of predicting the number of user visits for the websites or specific target rules. More specifically, [13] is a work for time series prediction like [14], [15]. In other words, they predict the total number of impression opportunities, and ad impression forecasting in this paper focuses on the ad-level forecasting that needs to consider impressions under the bid values.

# III. PROBLEM AND SYSTEM OVERVIEW

For a target advertisement ad, the number of impressions of next hour is given by:

$$
f (\beta) = \phi (\beta) \cdot X (\beta_ {0}) \tag {1}
$$

where $X(\beta_{0})$ is the number of benchmark impressions, which represents the impressions of the given ad in the next hour with the bid unchanged; $\phi(\beta)$ is the adjustment coefficient, which represents the rate of change of the number of impressions after bid change relative to the number of benchmark impressions; $\beta$ represents the new bid of ad and $\beta_{0}$ represents the original bid of ad.

# A. Estimation of Benchmark Impressions

As in equation (1), there are two commonly used methods for estimation of benchmark impressions $X(\beta_{0})$ :

- use the impressions of the last hour directly.   
- estimate the impressions in the next 1 hour use time series data from the impressions in the past few hours based on the time series model(eg. ARIMA, DLM [11]).

The actual coverage of these two methods is not ideal. The reason is that the time series of new ads cannot be obtained from historical data. Besides, the impressions of advertisement fluctuate greatly. For example, advertisements are often suspended due to the manual operation of advertisers and insufficient budget. In this case, the above two methods cannot effectively predict the result. At this point, we need to introduce a machine learning model XGBoost to improve the model's generalization ability for the missing values in the data [16].

As shown in Figure 2, we design various features of ad-level such as product type, advertiser type and so on. Moreover, some aggregation features are also used like average impressions of the current site in the past 3 hours.

# B. Estimation of Adjustment Coefficient

Formally, adjustment coefficient $\phi(\beta)$ can be expressed as the winning rate $\omega(\beta)$ of the new bid $\beta$ divided by the winning rate $\omega(\beta_{0})$ of the original bid $\beta_{0}$ :

$$
\phi (\beta) = \omega (\beta) / \omega (\beta_ {0}) \tag {2}
$$

We then obtain new equation for ad impression forecasting by:

$$
f (\beta) = \omega (\beta) / \omega (\beta_ {0}) \cdot X (\beta_ {0}) \tag {3}
$$

Therefore, it is a core step for our task to model the winning rate. When advertising system capture an impression opportunity, the winning bid of ad(The price at which the current ad can win) is equal to the actual winning eCPM of the auction divided by the current ad's eCPM and then multiplied by the current bid:

$$
\beta_ {w i n} = \frac {e C P M _ {w i n}}{e C P M _ {c u r r e n t}} \cdot \beta_ {0} \tag {4}
$$

Let $p$ denotes an impression opportunity and $M_p = (ad_1, ad_2, \cdots, ad_n)$ denotes the set of ad candidates of $p$ . Suppose $M_p$ 's eCPM scores are ranked from low to high and the winning ad is $ad_{n-1}(ad_n$ filtered out due to filtering rules), at this time $ad_{n-1}$ 's eCPM score is the winning eCPM. And $eCPM_{current}$ is the score of the given $ad$ . Therefore, by calculating the current ad's winning bids for all impression opportunities and sorting them from low to high, we obtain the list of winning bid $[\beta_{win_1}, \beta_{win_2}, \ldots, \beta_{win_n}]$ , based on which the cumulative distribution function (CDF) can be calculated:

$$
\omega (\beta) = P (x \leq \beta) \tag {5}
$$

According to equation (5), we can obtain the curve of the winning rate through statistics(as shown in Figure 3). However, there will be the following problems:

![](images/d0b428f1e6dfd80e0f3a2b4ea38c3f110a9526fed9fba8e6f932f4e40052267c.jpg)



Fig. 3. Winning rate against different bids.

- Outlier problem: There may be many industry support situations in the advertising delivery system. For example, before the winter and summer vacation, advertising in the education industry has an extra eCPM bonus; during the legal holiday, the game industry has an extra eCPM bonus. This will show that the winning bid calculated by equation (4) is particularly high. In this case, there will be outliers.   
- Sparse sampling problem: Because the data flow of the advertising system is too large, the collected reranking data is sampled at 1/256. This will cause an advertisement to have only a few winning bid points. For example, the winning rate curve may be formed by concatenating two straight lines (the number of winning bid points is 3), which does not fit the actual situation.

In order to solve the above two problems, in mathematics, the commonly used method is to model the winning rate curve. We use the RANSAC framework to fit the curve parameters and remove outliers. Figure 3 indicates that for an advertisement, when the bid is in the mid-to-low segment, there are a lot of ads competing with it, so the winning rate changes greatly because the bid increase(the slope is large); when the bid is in the high segment, there are fewer ads competing with it, so the change in the winning rate caused by the bid increase is relatively small (the slope is small). This is very similar to the cumulative distribution function of the gaussian distribution. Therefore, we model the winning rate curve through the gaussian distribution:

$$
\omega (\beta) = \int_ {0} ^ {\beta} f (x) d x \tag {6}
$$

The probability density function is:

$$
f (\beta) = \frac {1}{\sigma \sqrt {2 \pi}} e ^ {- \frac {1}{2} (\frac {\beta - \mu}{\sigma}) ^ {2}} \tag {7}
$$

Considering that the winning rate is 0 when the bid is 0, the cumulative distribution function is simplified to:

$$
\omega (\beta) = e r f \left(\frac {\beta - \mu}{\sigma \sqrt {2}}\right) \tag {8}
$$

![](images/ceaf9c46f2f08fb212f0473c2c8d529b8a575858fc889121619a08e3108bbdf2.jpg)



Fig. 4. Backtracking process of price adjustment coefficient.

$$
e r f (x) = \frac {2}{\sqrt {\pi}} \int_ {0} ^ {x} e ^ {- t ^ {2}} d t \tag {9}
$$

Here, by means of the Taylor expansion technique for the Gauss error function $erf(x)$ :

$$
e r f (x) = \frac {2}{\sqrt {\pi}} \left(x - \frac {x ^ {3}}{3} + \frac {x ^ {5}}{1 0} - \frac {x ^ {7}}{4 2} + \dots\right) \tag {10}
$$

According to the RANSAC algorithm, the parameters in equation (8) can be calculated. After that, the bid adjustment coefficient curve can be modeled according to equation (2). In this way, we can solve the problems of outliers and sparse sampling mentioned above. Using the above method to calculate the bid adjustment coefficient, each advertisement needs at least 3 sampling points to participate in the fitting process. However, due to the sampling ratio of 1/256, the reranking data is relatively sparse. This leads to sparse sampling points of some advertisements, which cannot meet the requirements of the RANSAC algorithm. Therefore, we trace back the bid adjustment coefficient that is closest to the current advertisement through the method shown in Figure 4, which greatly improves the coverage. specifically speaking, if the feature aid of the given advertisement $ad_j$ cannot be found in the sample data, we will search for an ads list $ad_1, ad_2, ..., ad_n$ based on the four features of $ad_j$ : product\_id, product\_type, optimization\_goal and placement\_group\_id, and then calculate the average bid adjustment coefficient of these ads as the adjustment coefficient of the $ad_j$ . Similarly, if these four features product\_id, product\_type, optimization\_goal and placement\_group\_id cannot find any ads, we will search for ads list based on the four features advertiser\_id, product\_type, optimization\_goal and placement\_group\_id. The rest can be operated in the same manner.

# C. System Framework

In this section, we build a system ACMIF that uses ad impression algorithms to provide complete services. As shown in Figure 5, the overall engineering architecture is divided into data layer, feature layer, algorithm layer and business layer.

1) Data Layer: The data layer is responsible for directly accepting upstream data, including reranking data flow and impression data flow. Reranking data flow records the information needed during real-time bidding such as winning eCPM, bid and the current eCPM of the candidate ads that entered the reranking layer. Due to a large amount of data in reranking data flow, 1/256 of the data is sampled during storage. It is used when estimating the adjustment coefficient. Impression data flow is used to forecast benchmark impressions.   
2) Feature Layer: Feature layer takes charge of further processing the data flow in the data layer:

- After obtaining the winning eCPM, bid and the current eCPM of each impression opportunity in equation (4) through the reranking data flow, winning bid points of advertisement can be calculated at this time.   
- The system first quickly aggregates the impression data of each advertisement in the minute-level impression data flow, and then further obtains the impressions $X(\beta_0)$ of

one hour. At the same time, we also get the features needed for XGBoost model training and prediction.

3) Algorithm Layer: The algorithm layer deploys the model we used for estimation. Based on the data obtained in the feature layer, we can fit parameters using the RANSAC algorithm and equation (8). At this point, the winning rate curve can be modeled.   
4) Business Layer: Our algorithm provides services to multiple businesses, such as bid simulator, high-value advertising screening, large client advertising diagnosis and programmatic advertising bid adjustment. Our algorithm shows the advertiser how changes to bid might change the number of impressions, which is also called bid simulator $^{1}$ . Through the ad impression forecasting service, we traverse all the ads and find out the ads whose impressions increase is more than twice the bid increase when the bid increases by 10%, 15% and 20%. These are the ads with great potential when bid increase. For large clients, the advertising diagnosis service obtains the impressions of the current ad under different bids in the next hour, and adjusts the bid of the ad in real-time. Similarly, The external DSP platform obtains the impressions of the current ad under different bids in the next hour through the business API, and helps them to programmatically adjust the bid value.

In business, in addition to ensuring the accuracy of estimation, it is also necessary to improve business interpretability. The ad impression forecasting service needs to ensure that the impressions of a given advertisement must increase monoton-

$^{1}$ https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6239130?hl=en ically after the bid increase. This is something that ordinary machine learning models or deep learning models cannot achieve, so we propose a RANSAC-based bid adjustment coefficient modeling method to ensure monotonicity.

# B. Evaluation Metrics

To demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework, we adopt the accuracy and coverage ratio as evaluation metrics. The detailed definitions are as follows:

a) Accuracy: :

$$
A c c u r a c y _ {\tau} = \frac {\# \{a d | a d \in A D \text {   and   } | R E | <   \tau \}}{\# A D} \tag {11}
$$

where AD is the set of all ads in the test dataset, ad belongs to AD and $|RE|$ means the absolute relative error of ad:

$$
R E = \frac {\# F o r e c a s t e d - \# A c t u a l}{\# A c t u a l} \tag {12}
$$

where #Forecasted and #Actual, for a given ad, represent the forecasted number of impressions and the actual number of impressions, respectively. Since it is almost impossible to accurately predict the actual number of an ad impression, we define the accuracy to measure the percentage of the number of ads that are relatively accurate estimation by different methods. Since the drastic changes of user visits and ads, the ad impression varies greatly over hours and days. According to the Tencent data from nearly 1,620,000 ads, 77.9%, 83.9% and 86.8% of the hour-level ad impressions vary greater than 20% in 1, 2 and 3 hours; 53.2%, 64.0% and 69.9% of impressions vary greater than 50% in 1, 2 and 3 hours. Therefore, in this paper, setting relative error to 20% or 50% makes sense in practice.

b) Coverage Ratio(CR): Our framework has been deployed online. As mentioned in §III-A, there is the problem of sparse sampling in the reranking data flow. Coverage rate represents the proportion of ads that can provide the service of ad impression forecasting to online ads of the site set in the past hour or the past 12 hours.

# C. Comparison with Existing Methods

We compare the evaluation metrics of our framework against the two following methods:

1) Training Hour Replay (THR): It is a search-based method proposed by Wang et al. [10], based on replaying the given advertisement ad historical impression opportunities over a period of time. [11] reduced it to Training Week Replay (TWR). Since our test data is in the next hour, we call it Training Hour Replay(THR). We retrieval impression opportunities that ad participated in training data logs. Because the bid of the advertisement may change, we need to recalculate the winning score of the ad according to the formula of eCPM, which is related to bid, pCTR, pCVR and the method of charging. By comparing the number of times the score is greater than the maximum eCPM score in the list, we finally get the impressions of the ad. Note that there is no record of new ads in the training data, leading to THR being unable to predict impressions for new ads. Since the pageviews in the reranking data flow have been sampled for 1/256, the wins obtained in THR multiplied by 256 will yield the final predicted value.

2) GMIF-Like Method (GMIFL): inspired by Nath et al. [11]. For display ad, it firstly finds the impression opportunities with the maximum eCPM score in the next hour, and then infers the probability of winning for each pageview. For the given ad $ad_{q}$ , in our framework, we have found available impression opportunities. After predicting the pCTR and pCVR, we directly get the new eCPM score of the impression opportunities, on the basis of which the adjustment coefficient can be calculated according to equation (2). The number of benchmark impressions is estimated using a Dynamic Linear Model(DLM). For new ads, we perform a backtracking process like Figure 4 to get impression opportunities and benchmark impressions of the given ad for estimation.

# V. CONCLUSION

In this work, we present a novel ad-level combined method for impression forecasting ACMIF to predict the number of impressions for a target ad with a given bid. We take the problem from sparse sampling of online advertising data flow into consideration, and model the benchmark impressions and adjustment coefficient to achieve better coverage and accuracy. We deployed the proposed framework in Tencent online display advertising system and evaluated the effectiveness and efficiency of the framework on a real-world dataset. Our experimental results demonstrate significant advantages over existing methods.
