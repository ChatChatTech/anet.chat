# Asymptotic throughput for large-scale wireless networks with general node density

Cheng Wang • Changjun Jiang • Xiang-Yang Li • Yunhao Liu

Published online: 28 July 2012 - Springer Science+Business Media, LLC 2012

Abstract We study the asymptotic throughput for a large-scale wireless ad hoc network consisting of n nodes under the generalized physical model. We directly compute the throughput of multicast sessions to unify the unicast and broadcast throughputs. We design two multicast schemes based on the so-called ordinary arterial road system and parallel arterial road system, respectively. Correspondingly, we derive the achievable multicast throughput by taking account of all possible cases of $n _ { s } = \omega ( 1 )$ and $1 \leq n _ { d } \leq n - 1$ , rather than only the cases

of $n _ { s } = \Theta ( n )$ as in most related works, where $n _ { s }$ and $n _ { d }$ denote the number of sessions and the number of destinations of each session, respectively. Furthermore, we consider the network with a general node density $\lambda \in [ 1 , n ]$ , while the models in most related works are either random dense network (RDN) or random extended network (REN) where the density is $\lambda = n$ and $\lambda = 1$ , respectively, which further enhances the generality of this work. Particularly, for the special case of our results by letting $\lambda = 1$ and $n _ { s } = \Theta ( n )$ , we show that for some regimes of $n _ { d } ,$ the multicast throughputs achieved by our schemes are better than those derived by the well-known percolation-based schemes.

Keywords Wireless ad hoc networks  Asymptotic throughput  Random networks  Generalized physical model  Multicast throughput

The second are called networking-theoretic bounds that assume that the signals received from nodes other than a particular transmitter are interference to be regarded as noise degrading the communication link. For the networking-theoretic capacity issue, there are generally three widely-used communication models: the protocol model [1], physical model [1], and generalized physical model [5] (also called Gaussian Channel model [6]).

In this paper, we study the multicast throughput for random networks under the generalized physical model. In [6, 7], for the purpose of achievable multicast throughput, all multicast schemes are designed by exploiting the connection between percolation theory and the way to scale the transmission ranges of nodes [8]. In those schemes, the routing backbones compose highways system, which are built based on bond percolation theory [9]. According to [8], for unicast session, the bottleneck of the percolationbased routing is always located on the highways. However, unlike unicast session, a peculiar question for multicast session arises, that is, as the regimes of $n _ { s }$ and $n _ { d }$ are changing, the bottleneck is probably displaced to the access paths to the highways ([6]), which possibly leads to a poorer multicast throughput. This paper intends to investigate the questions mentioned above, and the main contributions are two-fold.

1. We present the more general results. The generality is embodied in two aspects: first, we take account of all cases in terms of $n _ { s }$ and $^ { n } d ,$ without the specific assumptions of $n _ { s }$ and $n _ { d }$ as in most existing literatures; second, we consider the random networks with the general density $\lambda \in [ 1 , n ]$ , rather than only the random dense networks (RDN) [1, 7, 10–15] where $\lambda = n$ or random extended networks (REN) [4, 6, 8, 17, 16] where $\lambda = 1$ .

We devise the technique of parallel transmission scheduling to schedule the parallel arterial road system, which improves the final multicast throughput for some cases of the node density k. For the special cases of our result by letting k = 1 and $n _ { s } = \Theta ( n )$ , we show that the achievable multicast throughput under our schemes is better than that under the well-known percolationbased schemes [6].

# 2.1 Network model

We construct the random network by placing the nodes on the 2-dimensional plane according to a Poisson point process with the density $\lambda \in [ 1 , n ]$ , and focusing the attention to a square region $\mathcal { A } ( \lambda , n ) = [ 0 , \sqrt { n / \lambda } ] ^ { 2 }$ . To simplify the description, we assume the number of nodes is exactly n, without changing the final results in order sense, [8]. # 2.2 Scaling model

In the research of capacity scaling laws, there are two typical models according to scaling patterns of the network: extended scaling model and dense scaling model [4, 8, 18]. The major difference between the engineering implications of these two scaling models is related to the classical notions of interference-limitedness and coveragelimitedness. The dense networks tend to have dense deployments so that signals are received at the users with sufficient SINR (signal to Interference plus noise ratio) but the throughput is limited by interference among the simultaneous transmissions. That is, all nodes can communicate with each other with sufficient SINR, and the throughput can only be interference-limited. While, the extended networks tend to have sparse deployments so that the throughput is mainly limited by the ability to transmit signals to the users with sufficient SINR. That is, the source and destination pairs are at increasing distance from each other, so both interference limitation and power limitation can come into play. The network can be either coveragelimited or interference-limited.

Next, we examine the scaling characteristics of a random network with a general density $\lambda ,$ denoted by $\mathcal { N } ( \lambda , n )$ , according to the relation between k and n. First, we recall an existing result about random Euclidean minimal spanning tree (EMST) from [19]. In these works, the routing backbones compose highways system that is built based on percolation theory [23] and different from the arterial road system proposed in this paper.

Broadcast Capacity Keshavarz-Haddad etal [10] studied the broadcast capacity of an arbitrary network under the protocol model. They showed that the broadcast capacity is only of $\Theta ( 1 / n )$ . They studied the multicast capacity of random networks when the number of multicast sources is $n ^ { \varepsilon }$ for some $\varepsilon > 0$ , and the number of receivers per multicast session is $n ^ { 1 - \varepsilon }$ . # 7 Conclusion

Under the generalized physical model, we devise two new multicast schemes for random ad hoc networks with the general node density. Combining both schemes, we derive the multicast throughput according to the specific $n _ { s } , n _ { d }$ and k. We prove that in some regimes of $n _ { d }$ and $\lambda ,$ our schemes can achieve a better throughput than those based on percolation theory [6]. Our schemes can act as the complements of those percolation-based schemes, [6–8].
