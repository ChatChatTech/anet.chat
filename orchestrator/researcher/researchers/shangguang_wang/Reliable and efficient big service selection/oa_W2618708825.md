# Reliable and efficient big service selection

Ling Huang1 & Qinglin Zhao2 & Yan Li3 & Shangguang Wang1 & Lei Sun1 & Wu Chou4

Published online: 22 May 2017

\# Springer Science+Business Media New York 2017

Abstract Big services, both virtual (e.g., cloud services) and physical (e.g., public transportation), are evolving rapidly to handle and deal with big data. By aggregating services from various domains, big services adopt selection schemes to produce composite service solutions that meet customer requirements. However, unlike traditional service selection, a huge number of big services require some lengthy selection processes to improve the service reliability. In this paper, we propose an efficient big service selection approach based on the coefficient of variation and mixed integer programming that improves the solution in two senses: 1) minimizing the time cost and 2) maximizing the reliability. We tested our approach on real-world datasets, and the experimental results indicated that our approach is superior to others.

Keywords Service computing . Big service . Service selection . QoS

# 1 Introduction

Efficient analysis and processing of big data are imperative and require the implementation of various services across multiple domains and networks. Ideally, these services should speed up the big data processing and improve the service adaptability and extensibility despite the data diversity and uncertainty. Such interoperability across a large number of services, domains, and networks leads to a complex service ecosystem by necessity. Based on this service ecosystem of convergence and collaboration, i.e., a Bbig service,^ big data challenges can be addressed more effectively (Xiaofei et al. 2015).

Big services consisting of virtual, cloud, and physical services, such as public transportation, are rapidly evolving to handle and deal with big data. By aggregating these big services from various domains, IT software developers can adopt service selection schemes to produce composite service solutions that meet user requirements.

We consider big service selection to be similar to Web service selection. The latter problem is based on serviceoriented architecture (SOA) that satisfies the dynamic, complex, and cross-organizational business requirements through the creation of composite big services from basic individual services (Xiaofei et al. 2015). According to the SOA paradigm, service selection technology should ensure loose coupling and flexibility in the service design to support business applications distributed within and across organizational boundaries (Sun et al. 2014).

The quality of service (QoS) (e.g., the response time, reputation, and throughput) plays an important role in determining a big service design that meets the QoS constraints (Alrifai et al. 2010; Wang et al. 2011a). Some conventional and notable approaches to service selection include hybrid (Alrifai and Risse 2009), GA (Canfora et al. 2005a, b), replanning (Canfora et al. 2005a, b), CAR & AR (Hwang et al. 2008), MIP (Ardagna and Pernici 2007), and heuristic (Yu et al. 2007). Although these approaches have been shown to perform well in Web service environments, they may not be efficient when applied to big services owing to the following two factors:

1) The uncertainty of the QoS is much higher for big services than for conventional services. For example, unlike conventional service environments, the response time of the big service changes over time. Thus, a reliable big service cannot be provided to users due to the dynamic big data environment. In general, services participating in big service selection are often widely distributed in multiple domains, networks, and the cyber-physical world (i.e., big services come from different organizations/ systems and run on different operation platforms). As such, any slight change in location, network, environment, or other related aspects will affect the QoS consistency of these big service candidates, which we call the BQoS uncertainty.^ A big service with consistently good QoS is typically more reliable than a big service with a large variance in QoS. Hence, the QoS uncertainty should be considered as an essential factor for reliable big service selection. Note that the QoS uncertainty also impacts conventional service selection schemes, but its effect on big service selection should be much bigger owing to the expanded service domain.

2) Big service selection requires a higher time cost than conventional service selection schemes. During the big service selection process, various services across different domains collect, store, and analyze big data, taking the information derived from the data, and applying the information to service selection and quick responses to construct customer-oriented service solutions. In particular, for traffic events such as accidents and traffic control issues, the selection needs to be based on real-time traffic information (Xiaofei et al. 2015). An obvious approach to obtaining the best result is to enumerate all possible compositions, which will lead to a NP-hard problem (Shangguang et al. 2011). Even for conventional service selection schemes, reducing the time cost is still a research issue. When the number of service candidates is very small, this time cost is acceptable. However, with the rapid increase in big services from cloud computing (Ren et al. 2015), the Internet of Things, social networks, etc., enumerating all possible candidates becomes

unrealistic and incurs a high time cost. Therefore, to reduce the computational time and search space for service candidates satisfying the reliability requirement, has become an essential principle for big service selection in a big data environment.

Conventional service selection schemes also consider the time cost and reliability, but these two issues are not significant in this environment. However, because of the characteristics and structures of big services, the time cost and reliability are critically important in big service environments. Therefore, in contrast to the previous work, in this paper we focus on the QoS uncertainty and time cost to propose a novel big service selection approach for finding the best reliable composite service for users while minimizing the time cost. As noted, the QoS uncertainty of a big service can lower its service reliability. Hence, we adopted a coefficient of variation framework to compute the QoS uncertainty; then, unreliable big services are pruned, and highly reliable big services are determined for big service composite systems. We applied mixed integer programming (MIP) to big service selection for yielding the best composite service. We tested our approach on some real-world services, and the results showed that our approach found the most reliable composite service with a low time cost.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the concepts related to big service selection and related work. Section 3 describes our approach in detail, including QoS uncertainty computing and the big service selection algorithm. Section 4 presents the experimental results to demonstrate the better performance of our approach compared to other approaches. Finally, Section 5 concludes the paper.

# 2 Related concepts and work

# 2.1 Related concepts

In this section, we explain some concepts related to big service selection.

Let $S = \{ s _ { 1 } , s _ { 2 } , \ldots , s _ { n } \} ( n = 1 , 2 , 3 , \ldots )$ denote a composite big service to achieve a particular function that meets the user’s requirements. S is obtained by combining multiple big service candidates selected from various big service classes.

Let $s _ { i } \in S ( i = 1 , 2 , 3 , . . . ) ( 1 \leq i \leq n )$ denote a big service class that consists of a number of big service candidates $s _ { i } = \{ s _ { i 1 } ,$ , $s _ { i 2 } , . . . s _ { i l } \}$ , where $l ( l = 1 , 2 , 3 , . . . )$ represents the number of big service candidates with different QoSs.

For a single big service, its QoS attributes can be divided into two categories: negative (the smaller the better, such as the response time and price) and positive (the larger the better, such as reliability and availability). Because positive attribute values can easily be converted into negative attribute values, we only considered negative QoS attributes (Zeng et al. 2003; Zeng et al. 2004; Shangguang et al. 2011).

The QoS utility function is used to calculate the global QoS attribute values of each candidate in each big service class to rank them. The QoS utility function that we used is similar to that of Zeng et al. (2003, 2004) and Shangguang et al. (2011). When selecting a big service, the global QoS constraints are often used to find the best composite big services. # 2.2 Related work

The concept of big services was first proposed by Xiaofei et al. (2015). However, little work has been done on big service selection. Because the big service selection problem is similar to conventional service selection, here we review some conventional and notable works.

Conventionally, QoS-aware service selection approaches (Benatallah et al. 2002; Zeng et al. 2003; Liu et al. 2004) typically ignore the user’s end-to-end global QoS constraints. Most are inefficient and require a great deal of computation time. Many globe optimal service selection approaches (Ardagna and Pernici 2007; Zeng et al. 2003, 2004) have been proposed to find the best optimal composite service under the user’s global QoS constraints. For example, Zeng et al. (2003) focused on dynamic and quality-driven service selection by attaching importance to multiple attributes and global constraints. They first proposed QoS aggregation functions based on weighting QoS attributes for service selection. Soon after, they (Zeng et al. 2004) proposed a middleware platform for a composite Web service by obtaining the maximum of utility functions over QoS attributes while satisfying the user’s requirements. Because these approaches enumerate all possible combinations of service candidates, they cannot efficiently handle the excessive time cost as the number of Web services is increased. Wang et al. (2011b) were the first to propose an adaptive service selection scheme based on particle swarm optimization that reduces the time cost from seconds to milliseconds.

Many near-to-optimal service selection approaches have been proposed to reduce the computation time (Alrifai and Risse 2009; Lianyong et al. 2010). For example, Alrifai and Risse (2009) proposed an efficient approach to QoS-based service composition that combines global optimization with local selection methods to select composite services. This approach is realized by using MIP to find the optimal decomposition of global QoS constraints into local constraints and then using local selection to find the optimal composite services. This is useful for applications with dynamic changes and realtime requirements. Lianyong et al. (2010) converted the global QoS constraints into local constraints to selecting some of the services in each class and then used MIP to enumerate all possible composites to select a near-to-optimal one.

The skyline service (Alrifai et al. 2010; Yu and Bouguettaya 2010; Benouaret et al. 2011) and QoS dependence service (Barakat et al. 2012; Yuzhang et al. 2013) were proposed to shorten the computation time of service selection. Alrifai et al. (2010) focused on selecting skyline services as candidates for a QoS-based Web service composite, which helped reduce the number of service candidates. Yu and Bouguettaya (2010) proposed a skyline computation approach for service selection that enables users to optimally and efficiently access sets of service as an integrated service package. Barakat et al. (2012) presented a correlation-aware service selection approach to handle QoS dependencies among Web services and improve the composite quality. This approach first models the quality dependencies among Web services and then prunes uninteresting composites from the search space before selection. This produces the best utility and remains efficient even when a considerable number of correlations exist among services. Yuzhang et al. (2013) considered the QoS-aware service selection problem in the presence of service-dependent QoS constraints and userprovided topological and QoS constraints. This approach effectively handles the problem of service-dependent QoS by directly integrating it into the composition process rather than afterwards. Yuzhang et al. demonstrated that this approach can significantly improve the performance in real-world scenarios with complex service and QoS dependencies.

# 3 Our proposed approach

In this paper, we propose an efficient big service selection approach that uses QoS and uncertainty computing, which is also known as CVMIP. CVMIP contains three phases. The first phase is QoS uncertainty computing, which adopts the coefficient of variation to transform the QoS values into a qualitative concept that represents the degree of QoS uncertainty of a big service. The second phase is uncertain services filtering to prune uncertain service candidates based on the qualitative concept in order to ensure the reliability of the composite service while reducing the search space of the big service selection. The final phase is big service selection, where a new and better MIP algorithm is used to select the best reliable composite service with a shorter computation time.

# 3.1 QoS uncertainty computing

The quantitative QoS values are normalized into the domain [0, 1], which is convenient for data processing and uniform QoS attribute values. Then, information theory and variance theory are employed to compute the QoS uncertainty, where QoS quantitative values are transformed into two QoS qualitative concepts. According to the two qualitative concepts, a big service with consistently good QoS can then be distinguished from other big services. As an illustration, we first give the following relevant concepts.

# 1) Data normalization

The normalization process limits the values within a certain range (e.g., [0, 1]) and is convenient for QoS utility functions. Data normalization means that the original QoS values are scaled proportionally. There are many normalization methods, such as linear conversion, logarithmic conversion, and cotangent conversion. We adopted linear conversion to normalize the QoS values. The specific formula is as follows:

$$
y = (x - \text { Minvalue }) / (\text { Maxvalue } - \text { Minvalue }) \tag {4}
$$

where x and y represent the corresponding values before and after QoS data conversion, respectively, and Maxvalue and Minvalue represent the maximum and minimum, respectively, of the original data.

# 2) Coefficient of variation

In probability theory, the coefficient of variation is a normalized measure of the dispersion degree of a probability distribution. It can help with comparing the discrete degree of two or more random variables, especially when their measurement units or averages are not the same. In general, the greater the uncertainty of a variable, the larger the coefficient of variation (i.e., the greater the disorder of its corresponding random variable). We consider the real-world historical QoS values for a big service as a discrete random variable and employ the concept of the coefficient of variation to filter the uncertain big services. The coefficient of variation is defined below.

Definition 1 (coefficient of variation) Let X be a random variable where $\{ X _ { I } , X _ { 2 } , . . . , X _ { n } \}$ is the range of X. Then, the coefficient of variation can be obtained as follows:

$$
C V = S / \overline {{X}} \times 100 \% \tag{5}
$$

where

$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{c} \overline {{{X}}} = \frac {1}{n} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} x _ {i} \\ S = \sqrt {\frac {1}{n - 1} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} \left(x _ {i} - \overline {{{X}}}\right) ^ {2}} \end{array} \right. \tag {6}
$$

where S represents the standard deviation of X and $\overline { { X } }$ represents the average of X.

In this manner, the coefficient of variation can help reduce the search space for the big service selection and ensure the reliability of the composite service.

# 3.3 Service selection algorithm

By using QoS uncertainty computing and uncertain service filtering, some unreliable and redundant service candidates are pruned. A big service selection solution has to be found to determine the most reliable service of each class under global QoS constraints in a small search space. We used an MIP algorithm to solve the optimization problem of big service selection as follows:

$$
\operatorname{Max} F (S) = \sum_ {k = 1} ^ {r} \frac {Q . C V _ {k} ^ {\max} - \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} \sum_ {j = 1} ^ {l} x _ {i j} \cdot q _ {k} \left(s _ {i j}\right) \cdot c v _ {k} \left(s _ {i j}\right)}{\left(Q . C V _ {k} ^ {\max} - Q . C V _ {k} ^ {\min}\right)} \cdot \omega_ {k} \tag {7}
$$

where

$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{l} C V _ {i, k} ^ {\max} = \underset {\forall s _ {i j} \in s _ {i}} {\max} c v _ {k} (s _ {i j}) \\ C V _ {i, k} ^ {\min} = \underset {\forall s _ {i j} \in s _ {i}} {\min} c v _ {k} (s _ {i j}) \end{array} \right. \tag {8}
$$

$$

# 4 Experiments

We conducted experiments and compared our proposed approach against other state-of-the-art efficient selection approaches in terms of the computation time, reliability, and performance. We also studied the parameters of our approach (i.e., CVMIP).

# 4.1 Experiment setup

A real-world big service dataset would have been ideal for performance evaluation. Unfortunately, there is no big service dataset available. Hence, it was very difficult to construct an objective and scientific experiment. We had to devise a near-toobjective experiment to evaluate our approach, so we used two types of datasets. The first was a real-world Web service QoS dataset (Zheng et al. 2010; Yilei et al. 2011) named WSDream. It contains nearly 2 million real-world QoS Web service invocation records, and each record contains two QoS attributes (i.e., response time and throughput) collected by 339 service users on 5825 Web services. Because we only considered the sequential composition model, we used the throughput as the price attribute in our experiment. WSDream also contains information on these 5825 Web services and 339 users. In order to ensure that the experimental results of CVMIP were not biased by WSDream, we also evaluated our approach with a synthetic dataset (named Random) that contained 10,000 big services with two QoS attributes. We employed RAND() to create the data for the response time and throughput.

# 4.5 Study of parameters

# 4.5.1 Parameter CV

In this experiment, we considered the computation time when changing the parameter CV. Setting CV to 30 represents selecting 30% of the services as effective candidates.

As shown in Fig. 7, the computation time of our approach for the WSDream dataset increased when CV was increased from 10 to 90. This demonstrated that the computation time increases with CV because the big service selection search space increases rapidly. The performance of our approach always improved with increasing CV in Fig. 8, where Fig. 9 shows that the reliability of our approach also increased with CV for the WSDream dataset. Hence, no matter how CV is chosen, CVMIP performs well regardless of the CV value.

# 4.5.2 Parameter ω

In this experiment, we considered how the user preference weight ω affects the reliability of big service selection. ω represents the user’s requests for each QoS attribute and is very important for big service selection. We fixed the number of service candidates to 500, and the weight of the response time was varied from 0.125 to 0.875. Thus, the weight of the price varied from 0.125 to 0.875.

Figures 10, 11, and 12 are the results with two datasets. Regardless of how ω was allocated, CVMIP performed well. Moreover, when ω was allocated to the average value, CVMIP obtained the lowest computation time.

# 5 Conclusions

In this paper, we presented a reliable and efficient big service selection approach based on the coefficient of variation. We first used the coefficient of variation to compute the QoS uncertainty to filter out services with high uncertainty and reduce the search space. We then used MIP to select a near-to-optimal composite service with high reliability with a reduced computation time. We evaluated our approach using both real-world and randomly generated datasets. The experimental results showed that our approach found the best composite service with a shorter computation time and higher reliability than other approaches. In future work, we will improve the efficiency and effectiveness of our approach to big service selection on the basis of user preferences and privacy (Fu et al. 2015; Xia et al. 2014). We will further evaluate our approach by using a real-world big service dataset.
