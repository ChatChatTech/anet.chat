# ABSTRACT

Session-based recommendation (SBR) predicts the next item based on anonymous sessions. Traditional SBR explores user intents based on ID collaborations or auxiliary content. To further alleviate data sparsity and cold-start issues, recent Multimodal SBR (MSBR) methods utilize simplistic pre-trained models for modality learning but have limitations in semantic richness. Considering semantic reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLM), we focus on the LLM-enhanced MSBR scenario in this paper, which leverages LLM cognition for comprehensive multimodal representation generation, to enhance downstream MSBR. Tackling this problem faces two challenges: i) how to obtain LLM cognition on both transitional patterns and inherent multimodal knowledge, ii) how to align both features into one unified LLM, minimize discrepancy while maximizing representation utility. To this end, we propose a multimodal LLMenhanced framework TPAD, which extends a distillation paradigm to decouple and align transitional patterns for promoting MSBR. TPAD establishes parallel Knowledge-MLLM and Transfer-MLLM, where the former interprets item knowledge-reflected features and the latter extracts transition-aware features underneath sessions. A transitional pattern alignment module harnessing mutual information estimation theory unites two MLLMs, alleviating distribution discrepancy and distilling transitional patterns into modal representations. Extensive experiments on real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework.

# 1 INTRODUCTION

Session-based recommendation (SBR) [17, 71, 72] is a practical and challenging scenario, where users’ profiles and long-term behaviors are unavailable due to privacy policies or non-logged-in nature. Thereby, exploring short-term sequential patterns within anonymous sessions remains the crux of SBR. Initial attempts rely on ID-based methods [32, 53, 57, 66], which are restricted by sparsity and cold-start problems [6, 69]. To mitigate these problems, contentbased methods [24, 27, 40] utilize auxiliary information, e.g., item attributes, knowledge graphs, to enhance representations with rich semantics. Recently, several studies investigate Multimodal SBR (MSBR) pipelines, i.e., MMSBR [82] and DIMO [83], which fuse various modalities to portray comprehensive user interests. However, the performance of existing MSBR models is bottlenecked by the low quality of multimodal representations which are often extracted by inferior pre-trained models.

The recent breakthrough of Large Language Models [10, 45, 86, 87] sheds light on solving such bottlenecks given their extensive knowledge and sophisticated reasoning capabilities, where two paradigms obtain growing attention: LLM-centric and LLMenhanced approaches. Specifically, LLM-centric approaches [14, 56, 64, 79] directly reform LLMs as recommenders with designed instructions. Considering the expansive decision space involved in generating the next item and the accompanying issue of hallucinations, these works commonly reformulate the recommendation task as a binary classification problem (e.g., TALLRec [3]), or a multiple-choice question answering problem (e.g., LLaRA [33]). Although LLMs can work under well-curated datasets and toy settings, they inevitably suffer from accuracy drop and inference inefficiency in practical scenarios, when the input token length increases due to session growth [21] or the all-ranking  setting [52]. Given these limitations, researchers start to prioritize another branch of methods, i.e., LLM-enhanced approaches [16, 35, 38], which treat LLMs as powerful embedding models to interpret item content, producing expressive representations to downstream recommenders. These studies leverage LLM’s superior capability in representation learning rather than preference modeling, circumventing problems caused by lengthy sequences and delegating the all-ranking tasks to conventional recommenders. For instance, NoteLLM2 [77] integrates language models and vision encoders to construct multimodal embeddings for item-to-item recommendation, while Molar [46] generates unified item representations with a post-alignment mechanism. Even though these works pioneer the study of LLMenhanced paradigm, they still encounter significant constraints when adapted to MSBR due to the following challenges:

C1: How to obtain LLM cognition on both transitional patterns and inherent multimodal knowledge. Multimodal knowledge represents item intrinsic features, while transitional patterns indicate sequential behavioral cues suggesting ID collaborations. As shown in Figure 1.C1, obvious distributional inconsistency exists between the knowledge-reflected feature space and transition-aware feature space. Thus, discriminating their heterogeneity is essential to facilitate holistic representation learning in LLM. Previous studies [46, 68, 76] employ LLM to only model the knowledge-reflected features, or directly generate both features in one shared space. Such coarse-grained modeling (i) leads to an incomplete LLM perception of dynamic and static item characteristics, (ii) homogeneous representation generation and poor generalizability. Multimodal knowledge and transition patterns are intertwined in sessions, making it difficult to disentangle them in LLM comprehension.

C2: How to align both features into one unified LLM, minimize discrepancy while maximizing representation utility. On the one hand, session data contains diverse transitional patterns, which exhibit hierarchical cooperation of item contents. As shown in Figure 1.C2, users’ evolving intents within a session are externalized as transitional patterns in distinct semantic contexts. And item transitions exhibit both intra- and inter-session [60, 72], further complicating the extraction of patterns. On the other hand, due to distributional bias between knowledge-reflected and transition-aware features, roughly incorporate both potentially results in negative transfer [52] that harms pre-existing semantic LLM cognition. Previous works [49, 74] include entire sessions into the prompt, which (i) ignore discriminating diverse patterns existing in intra-session local and inter-session global transitions, (ii) fail to selectively absorb transition-aware features while preserving knowledge-reflected features. Realizing a balanced integration in LLM of transitional patterns into item multimodal representation remains challenging.

To tackle these challenges, we follow the LLM-enhanced paradigm and propose a novel framework that realizes Transitional Pattern Distillation (TPAD) to Large Language Model for MSBR. TPAD proposes a multimodal distilling paradigm to disentangle and align transitional patterns into multimodal representation learning, ultimately enhancing the performance of downstream MSBR. To address C1, we propose a dual-tower MLLM structure, i.e., Knowledge-MLLM (K-MLLM) and Transfer-MLLM (T-MLLM), which disentangles the learning process of static and dynamic item features. K-MLLM is guided by item modality reasoning prompts to cognize knowledge-reflected features within static modalities, while T-MLLM is instructed by session transition reasoning prompts to extract transition-aware features within dynamic session context. To overcome C2, we develop the Transitional Pattern Alignment (TPA) and the distillation paradigm, which achieve comprehensive incorporation of transitional patterns into multimodal representations. The TPA module employs mutual information estimation (MIE) theory in two aspects: (a) Minimize the upper bound of dependencies between knowledge-reflected and transition-aware features. (b) Maximize the lower bound of consistency between distilled transitional patterns and collaborative features. The distillation paradigm can be divided into two stages, i.e., Knowledge Acquisition and Transition Distillation. The first stage emphasizes the independent training of K-MLLM to construct an initial multimodal knowledge comprehension space. The second stage connects T-MLLM and K-MLLM, while ensuring the preservation of knowledge-reflected features, distilling fine-grained patterns into the knowledge space.

The contributions are summarized: (1) We introduce a novel LLMenhanced MSBR, which stimulates LLM potentials in integrating transitional pattern extraction with multimodal learning. (2) We develop a dual-tower MLLM structure that decouples transition-aware and knowledge-reflected features. (3) We propose a multimodal distillation pipeline, achieving effective transfer of transitional patterns into multimodal representations. (4) We conduct extensive experiments to demonstrate the effectiveness of TPAD.

# 2 RELATED WORK

Session-based Recommendation. Early ID-based methods adopt Markov chains [53] or various deep neural networks, like RNNs [29, 39], GNNs [55, 61, 71, 73], and attention mechanisms [7, 54], to extract dynamic user intents. To alleviate data sparsity and cold start problems, content-based SBR incorporates auxiliary information such as attributes [5, 27, 81], knowledge graphs [6, 47], and descriptive texts [9, 18], promoting performance to some extent. To further enhance representations, recent works [82, 83] leverage multimodal learning to exploit multifaceted session patterns. MSBR [82] applies a hierarchical transformer to fuse modalities, while DIMO [83] disentangles modality effects in a semantic space. However, compared with LLM-based methods, these studies demonstrate relatively constrained capability in complex semantic modeling.

LLMs for Session-based Recommendation. LLMs exhibit promising potential in developing recommenders [34, 48, 85] with sophisticated reasoning abilities and extensive knowledge. LLM-based recommendation falls into two types [70], i.e., prompt-based and fine-tuning-based. Prompt-based methods [11, 19, 37, 38] employs in-context learning or prompt optimization to engage LLMs as recommenders without re-training. Fine-tuning-based methods [2, 21, 30, 31, 76] update LLM parameters by training with domainspecific knowledge, making them suitable for particular tasks. For LLM-based SBR, there are two paradigms, i.e., LLM-centric and LLM-enhanced. LLM-centric SBR [15, 56, 65] positions LLMs as the core recommenders, utilizing either prompts or fine-tuning for nextitem predictions. For instance, LLMGR [15] employs LLMs to align textual and graph-structured data. Re2LLM [65] develops a reflective model to utilize reinforcement learning for enhancing LLMs. However, due to the slow inference resulted by long prompts, these models exhibit severe inefficiency and fail to realize all-ranking tasks. In contrast, LLM-enhanced SBR [23, 50] complements SBR with semantic comprehension on representation learning. For example, LLM4SBR [50] introduces a framework leveraging LLMs for intent inference. LLM-BRec [23] utilizes LLMs to generate user profiles for preference modeling. Considering the low efficiency and adaptability of LLM-centric pipelines, we further develop LLMenhanced SBR. Compared with previous studies that sorely deploy LLM textual processing proficiency, our framework harnesses LLMs to exploit comprehensive session patterns under multimodal forms, addressing a critical leap in this domain.

Multimodal Large Language Models. Multimodal large language models (MLLM) [22, 42, 44, 78] emerge to enable richer contextual cognition with various modalities. Early research [36, 67] combines separate modal-specific encoders and aligns through joint spaces. Recent studies [12, 28, 43] integrate modality-specific encoders using cross-modal attention. Research on MLLM-based recommender [26, 41, 51, 75] remains in its nascent stages, and there are two construction pipelines. The first line [49, 74] integrates multimodal knowledge to enhance LLM-centric recommenders, promoting user interest modeling for prediction. The second line [46, 58, 77] employs MLLM to augment multimodal learning, providing informative embeddings for downstream tasks. However, when adapting MLLM into SBR, studies in the first line suffer from inference computational costs while the second line, such as NoteLLM-2 [77] and Molar [46], primarily optimize the exploration of item inherent features and overlooks critical multi-order collaborations. Contrastingly, TPAD builds parallel MLLMs to capture knowledge-reflected and transition-aware features, and distill transitional patterns into multimodal representations.

# 3.2 An overview of TPAD

TPAD reforms LLM-enhanced MSBR with a pattern distillation paradigm, to drive LLMs to adaptively integrate transitional patterns into modal embeddings. The framework is presented in Figure. 2, which consists of three modules: (i) Knowledge-MLLM (K-MLLM) serves as the generator for multimodal representations, utilizing attribute inference prompts to bestow on LLM compressing proficiency of knowledge-reflected features. (ii) Transfer-MLLM (T-MLLM) interprets multi-order transitions with recursive reasoning instructions, decoupling transition-aware features. (iii) Transitional Pattern Alignment (TPA) bridges K-MLLM and T-MLLM, utilizing mutual influence estimation to resolve distribution discrepancy among sequential patterns. From the pipeline level, the paradigm is divided into: Knowledge Acquisition Stage and Transition Distillation Stages. The first stage depicts item intrinsic features with dependently training K-MLLM. The second stage extracts multi-order transition with T-MLLM and distills transition-aware semantic understanding into K-MLLM with progressive alignment.

# 3.5 Transitional Pattern Alignment

To enable K-MLLM producing generalizable multimodal embeddings, we propose a Transitional Pattern Alignment (TPA) accompanying the transition distillation stage. The alignment aims to (i) transfer context-aware sequential dependencies into static item multimodal representations, and (ii) enhance a balanced LLM cognition on transition-aware and knowledge-reflected features.

Dual-level Feature Disentanglement. Considering item embeddings as a unification of knowledge-reflected and transition-aware features, we first decompose this dual-level impact. In the distillation stage, we initialize K-MLLM with $K { \cdot } M L L \bar { M } ^ { ( 0 ) }$ and retrieve item visual and textual item summary embeddings, $i . e . , e _ { v } ^ { \kappa }$ and $\boldsymbol { e } _ { t } ^ { \kappa } .$ . We first project item multimodal summary embeddings into two independent feature spaces:

$$
\boldsymbol {z} _ {o} ^ {\kappa} = D _ {H} ^ {o} (\boldsymbol {e} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}), \boldsymbol {w} _ {o} ^ {\kappa} = D _ {L} ^ {o} (\boldsymbol {e} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}), o \in \{v, t \}, \tag {5}
$$

where $D _ { H } ^ { o }$ and $D _ { L } ^ { o }$ are modal-specific multi-layer perceptron. $D _ { H } ^ { o }$ serves as the transition-aware feature decoder while $D _ { L } ^ { o }$ as the knowledge-reflected feature decoder. Then we propose a Hierarchical Mutual Information Estimator (HMIE) to achieve disentanglement. We utilize a variational distribution $q _ { \theta } ( \pmb { w } _ { o } ^ { \kappa } | z _ { o } ^ { \kappa } )$ to approximate the conditional distribution $p ( w _ { o } ^ { \kappa } | z _ { o } ^ { \kappa } )$ , then the MI can be constrained by a variational contrastive log-ratio upper bound [8]:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathcal {I} _ {\text {CLUB}} \left(\boldsymbol {z} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}; \boldsymbol {w} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}\right) = \mathbb {E} _ {p \left(\boldsymbol {z} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}, \boldsymbol {w} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}\right)} \left[ \log q _ {\theta} \left(\boldsymbol {w} _ {o} ^ {\kappa} \mid \boldsymbol {z} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}\right) \right] \tag {6} \\ - \mathbb {E} _ {p (z _ {o} ^ {\kappa})} \mathbb {E} _ {p (w _ {o} ^ {\kappa})} \left[ \log q _ {\theta} (w _ {o} ^ {\kappa} | z _ {o} ^ {\kappa}) \right]. \\ \end{array}
$$

$$
\mathcal {L} _ {M I U} = \widetilde {\mathcal {I}} _ {\mathrm{CLUB}} = \frac {1}{N} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {N} [ \log q _ {\theta} (\boldsymbol {w} _ {o, i} ^ {\kappa} \mid \boldsymbol {z} _ {o, i} ^ {\kappa}) - \frac {1}{N} \sum_ {j = 1} ^ {N} \log q _ {\theta} (\boldsymbol {w} _ {o, j} ^ {\kappa} \mid \boldsymbol {z} _ {o, i} ^ {\kappa}) ].

$\begin{array} { r } { \mathcal { I } _ { \mathrm { M I N E } } ( z _ { o } ^ { \kappa } ; r _ { o } ^ { \kappa } ) = \operatorname* { s u p } _ { f : \Omega  \mathbb { R } } \mathbb { E } _ { p ( z _ { o } ^ { \kappa } , r _ { o } ^ { \kappa } ) } [ f ] - \log ( \mathbb { E } _ { \hat { p } ( z _ { o } ^ { \kappa } ) \hat { p } ( r _ { o } ^ { \kappa } ) } [ e ^ { f } ] ) , } \end{array}$ where the supremum is evaluated over all functions $f .$ Then we have the lower bound of mutual information:

$$
\mathcal {L} _ {M I L} ^ {h} = \mathbb {E} _ {p (\boldsymbol {z} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}, \boldsymbol {r} _ {o} ^ {\kappa})} [ f _ {\phi} (\boldsymbol {z} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}, \boldsymbol {r} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}) ] - \log (\mathbb {E} _ {p (\boldsymbol {z} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}) p (\boldsymbol {r} _ {o} ^ {\kappa})} [ e ^ {f _ {\phi} (\boldsymbol {z} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}, \boldsymbol {r} _ {o} ^ {\kappa})} ]),
$$

where $f _ { \phi }$ is the score function approximated by multilayer perception. Meanwhile, we input the zero-order of visual embeddings $\boldsymbol { e } _ { \boldsymbol { v } } ^ { 0 }$ and textual ${ \pmb e } _ { t } ^ { 0 }$ from $K - \bar { M } L L M ^ { ( 0 ) }$ into the projector $\overline { { e } } _ { o } = D _ { K } ^ { o } ( e _ { o } ^ { 0 } )$ , ?? ∈ $\{ v , t \}$ . $$
\mathcal {L} _ {M I L} ^ {s} = \mathbb {E} _ {p (\boldsymbol {w} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}, \overline {{\boldsymbol {e}}} _ {o} ^ {\kappa})} [ f _ {\phi} (\boldsymbol {w} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}, \overline {{\boldsymbol {e}}} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}) ] - \log (\mathbb {E} _ {p (\boldsymbol {w} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}) p (\overline {{\boldsymbol {e}}} _ {o} ^ {\kappa})} [ e ^ {f _ {\phi} (\boldsymbol {w} _ {o} ^ {\kappa}, \overline {{\boldsymbol {e}}} _ {o} ^ {\kappa})} ]).
$$

# 5 CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

We proposed an LLM-enhanced MSBR framework TPAD that develops a distillation paradigm of integrating transitional patterns into multimodal representation generation. TPAD contained two stages, i.e., the Knowledge Acquisition Stage and Transition Distillation Stage. The first stage extracted knowledge-aware features by instructing Knowledge-MLLM with item modality reasoning prompts and multimodal supervised fine-tuning. The second stage exploited transitional patterns through promoting Transfer-MLLM, and distilled transition-aware features into LLM comprehension. Extensive experiments demonstrated the effectiveness of TPAD. This work marks an initial step in leveraging LLM to advance MSBR, while leaving future work as: i) Introduce more modalities into LLM while ensuring the efficiency of joint modeling. ii) Harness feedback from downstream recommenders to guide LLM multimodal generation, establishing a paradigm of preference optimization.
