# Abstract

A multi-party service agreement refers to a textual agreement formulated under the participation of multiple parties, aiming to regulate the rights and responsibilities among them to facilitate their joint participation in a certain service or project. Traditional methods of executing agreements relying on centralized institutions may have potential trust issues. With the development of blockchain and smart contract technology, deploying textual agreements onto the blockchain for decentralized execution provides a new direction for addressing trust issues. However, the high complexity of textual agreements and cross-domain communication barriers result in difficulties and long cycles in smart contract development. To address the challenges faced in the development of smart contracts based on multi-party service agreements, this paper proposes a rapid and secure method for building smart contracts. Firstly, large language model(LLM) is employed to assist in extracting clause information from multi-party service agreements, and modeling the elements that can be expressed on the blockchain. Secondly, following the model-driven architecture (MDA), a smart contract reuse library is maintained to generate smart contract code. The results show that this method can automatically analyze the agreement text for blockchain representation, assist in manual modeling, generate the computation independent model(CIM), automatically convert it into the platform independent model(PIM), and further generate smart contract code. Through detailed case studies and verification of the code generation process, this paper demonstrates the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed method. The generated code, after passing security checks, can be successfully deployed and used, providing reliable technical support for the execution of textual agreements.

Keywords: Blockchain, Smart Contract, Blockchain Pattern, Model-Driven Architecture, Code Generation

# 1 Introduction

With the rapid development of economic globalization and information technology, various service businesses are showing increasingly rich features, involving the participation of multiple parties and multi-level cooperation, and the division of rights and obligations is also becoming increasingly complex. As a binding and normative cooperation agreement, the multi-party service agreement bears the important responsibility of regulating the rights and obligations of all parties and ensuring the smooth progress of cooperation. For example, the service level agreement (SLA) guarantees that the quality of service between service providers and customers reaches a certain level. The traditional centralized multi-party service agreement execution method is to supervise and enforce the contract through intermediaries or third-party trust institutions. In this way, during the execution process of the contract, the intermediary is responsible for ensuring that the contract is executed in accordance with the agreement and solving problems and disputes that arise during the cooperation process. However, they cannot solve the most critical trust issue, because the documents recording basic information may be tampered with maliciously. This centralized architecture relies too much on central institutions, and there are also obstacles to privacy protection in the agreement.

With the emergence of blockchain and smart contract technology, new solutions have emerged for trust issues in multi-party service agreements. Blockchain enables these agreements to be executed in a distributed manner, without requiring trust to be delegated to a centralized authority or mutual trust between parties. Blockchain [1] is a chronological data structure that connects multiple blocks containing events that cannot be tampered with. The blockchain network selects nodes that have permission to write records to the block content through consensus mechanisms to ensure that only one node can write information to the longest legal chain of the blockchain at the same time. Smart contract [2] is an automated contract based on blockchain technology. It is a piece of code written in a programming language to define and execute contract terms. Smart contracts enable reliable and automated transactions and cooperation without intermediaries. Due to the unique characteristics of smart contracts, an increasing number of multi-party service agreements are being operated through them, exerting profound influence on various industries such as financial services, real estate development, manufacturing, and healthcare. However, the widespread use of smart contracts also poses new challenges. The texts of multi-party service agreements are often lengthy and complex, involving numerous cross-domain collaborations. Developing suitable contract terms for blockchain integration into smart contracts presents certain difficulties. To address this issue, most current solutions adopt methods such as model-driven development, visual programming, and templates [3]. Nevertheless, these methods have not effectively resolved the problem of extracting contract terms for blockchain integration, and they rarely take into account both the execution process of service agreements and the modeling of agreement structures, which are two crucial aspects. Therefore, the generation of smart contracts for multi-party agreements based on blockchain needs to address the following challenges:

1. Challenge 1: How to efficiently obtain the necessary content for blockchain from multiple service agreement texts?   
2. Challenge 2: How to propose a model to express the content of multi-party service agreements on the blockchain, in order to reduce communication barriers between stakeholders and developers, and present multiparty service agreements in an intuitive and complete form. At the same time, support for the generation of smart contract code is provided.   
3. Challenge 3: How to design an automatic generation method for smart contract code that maximizes the automatic generation of smart contract code for multi-party service agreement models, reduces the development cycle of smart contracts, and reduces the possibility of errors during development.

To solve the above problems, this paper proposes a complete solution to generate smart contract code from multi-party service agreement text. Firstly, we define the blockchain patterns in multiparty service agreement, that is, which terms and content can be expressed in the form of smart contract on the blockchain. We extract the terms of text agreement with the help of LLM and obtain the blockchain elements through syntactic dependency analysis. Subsequently, we define the MDA model of multi-party service agreement, construct CIM based on the extracted blockchain elements, and support the conversion between models. The model includes the structure of the agreement and the execution process of the agreement terms. Finally, we maintain a multi-level smart contract reuse library corresponding to multiple blockchain patterns of multi-party service agreement, which supports the conversion of models into smart contract code. In order to illustrate the effectiveness of the method proposed in this paper, we select a real agreement as a case study. The contributions of this paper are as follows:

1. In accordance with the characteristics of multiparty service agreements, we have summarized the blockchain patterns and utilized LLM to extract textual content, determining which specific provisions and content can be expressed in the form of smart contracts on the blockchain.   
2. We have constructed an MDA meta-model specifically for the blockchain content of multiparty service agreements. This meta-model combines structural and process models, providing a more detailed expression of smart contracts and multi-party service agreements. In contrast, models proposed in other literature only focus on either structure or process, and are unable to express smart contracts across all domains.   
3. Leveraging a smart contract reuse library based on blockchain patterns, we propose an MDAbased algorithm for generating smart contract code.   
4. Developing an MDA-based smart contract generation framework and tool specifically tailored for multi-party service agreements.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: In Chapter 2, we introduce the main related work; in Chapter 3, we present the framework of our method and describe in detail the working process of each component, as well as the algorithms and models used; in Chapter 4, we analyze our method by referencing a real-world contract case; in Chapter 5, we discuss and compare the proposed method; finally, in Chapter 6, we summarize our method and provide conclusions.

# 2 Related work

# 2.1 Agreement text analysis and blockchain execution

Due to the emergence of blockchain and smart contract technology, businesses and academics have become increasingly interested in opportunities for automating and monitoring agreement execution. To formally represent legal concepts and their relationships in legal contracts, many researchers have proposed legal ontologies, but they are quite different from each other. Among them, Symboleo [4] is the most complete and detailed method, which demonstrates how to manually translate legal contracts into formal specifications through examples. In a subsequent study, the effectiveness of the proposed modeling method was verified [5]. Giancaspro et al. [6] have analyzed the potential legal and practical enforceability issues that may arise when combining smart contracts with traditional contract law, and have also examined the compatibility between smart contracts and traditional contract law. Currently, some scholars and communities such as Ethereum have been paying attention to automatically analyzing contract texts to form smart contract code. Several initial agreements have been proposed by the community [7, 8], which embed existing smart contracts on Ethereum into specific parts of agreements written using predefined templates and markup languages, thus enhancing traditional agreements with smart contracts. Fang Pengcheng et al. [8] mainly analyzed contracts in the financial field, summarized four types of sentences required to describe financial transactions, annotated a large number of contracts, and used a RoBERTa classifier for classification. They also designed the SmartIR language to abstract the four types of sentence intentions and bridge the gap between natural language sentences and smart contract statements.

Despite the potential of smart contracts in executing legal agreements, it remains a challenge for developers to craft smart contracts that accurately capture the core logic of legal agreements. Consequently, many researchers have focused on automatically extracting the core logic from legal texts and translating them into smart contract code. However, most current research efforts are concentrated on the extraction and generation of legal texts within specific domains, lacking a universal approach. The method of embedding smart contracts into specific parts of legal agreements using predefined templates merely treats smart contracts as external add-ons, leaving the crucial semantics of the agreements undigitized. Therefore, the approach proposed in this paper aims to address these issues by analyzing agreement texts across multiple domains and converting them into executable smart contracts.

# 2.2 Modeling and automatic generation of smart contracts

Currently, the automatic generation of smart contracts has received widespread attention, with a significant amount of work focused on converting the development requirements of smart contracts into models that are easy for humans to read and can be understood by computers. Mantas Jurgelaitis et al. [9] proposed a method based on Model-Driven Architecture (MDA), defining the CIM, PIM, and PSM metamodels for smart contracts as well as the model transformation process. This approach supports the generation of smart contract code in platforms such as Hyperledger Fabric Go and Ethereum’s Solidity language. Marek Skotnica et al. [10] introduced a method that models smart contracts as data models, process models, and form models. By analyzing these models, they developed a Solidity code generation algorithm for smart contracts using C#. The core of this method lies in using UML class diagrams to describe the metamodel of smart contracts, enabling the modeling and analysis of their structure and behavior. Flavio Corradini et al. [11] used a model-driven method and developed a framework named Chor-chain, which takes BPMN choreography as input and Solidity smart contract as output. Domain-Specific Models (DSM) employ Domain-Specific Languages (DSL) to describe system characteristics, supporting a higher level of abstraction than general-purpose modeling languages. Therefore, many scholars adopt DSM to support the automatic generation of smart contracts. T. Tateishi et al. [12] proposed a Domain-Specific Language for Smart Contracts (DSL4SC), which automatically generates smart contract code from legal contract documents. DSL4SC incorporates triple attributes: agreement, properties, and rules. This is a template-based approach to smart contract generation, where the parameter mapping between document templates and DSL4SC templates constitutes the smart contract template. ADICO [13] is another framework that follows the DSM approach for automatic smart contract generation. Human-readable contract statements are decomposed into ADICO components: attributes, definitions, goals, conditions, and alternatives. Wohrer et al. [14] designed a domain-specific language called Contract Modeling Language (CML) to describe the components of smart contracts and generate smart contract code based on CML. CML is close to natural language, with syntax that includes encoding abstractions and the automatic integration of common design patterns during code generation.

In addition to the automatic generation method based on model transformation, some research aims to quickly generate smart contracts through visual programming environments(VPE) or low-code development platforms. This method of generating smart contracts is generally targeted at developers or business personnel with some programming background, who do not need to write complete code and can quickly generate code through visual drag-and-drop code blocks or components. However, it may require additional coding for specific conditions. Contract Development Environment (CDE) [15] is a framework that uses Google’s Blockly to follow the VPE method. Google’s Blockly is an open source and web-based visual programming editor with user-friendly drag-and-drop building blocks to build programs. The Char-RNN model provides an interactive method for non-programmers to design smart contracts [16]. The system uses the Ethereum blockchain explorer to crawl the data set of smart contract programs, and employs the K-means++ clustering algorithm to complete domain-specific feature extraction. The underlying Char-RNN model learns these features and generates unified and basic functional code. The generated code includes transaction functions, ERC20 standard interface code, and SafeMath basic contract code, which are embedded into Google’s Blockly interactive UI controls where users can design and save their customized smart contracts.

Although the above-mentioned related work can automatically generate smart contracts within a certain scope, most model-based smart contract generation methods do not take into account the complexity of multi-party service agreement texts. At the same time, few models can simultaneously focus on both the structure and execution process of contracts. In addition, although visual programming environments can provide developers with a platform that can improve development efficiency, they cannot solve the complex problems faced by business personnel without programming skills when developing smart contracts.

# 3 Scheme

# 3.1 Overall Structure

As shown in Fig. 1, this is the overall architecture diagram of the model-driven smart contract generation method for multi-party service agreements. The system mainly consists of three modules: Blockchain Analysis Module, Service Agreement Modeling Module, and Code Generation Module.

Blockchain Analysis Module: Extract the Blockchain elements from the multi-party service agreement text, which are used to assist in the construction of the blockchain model for the multiparty service agreement.

Service Agreement Modeling Module: Based on the output from the blockchain analysis module, a model-driven architecture-based blockchain model for multi-party service agreements is constructed, with the realization of model transformation without any loss of blockchain semantics.

Code Generation Module: Maintain a multi-level smart contract reuse library based on the blockchain patterns, and convert the model into deployable and runnable complete smart contract code based on this reuse library.

# 3.2 Blockchain analysis method for agreement text

Considering the complexity of multi-party service agreements, not all clauses can be converted into smart contracts and deployed on the blockchain. Some clauses that have strong connections with the real world are difficult to translate into code. For example, a clause in a house lease contract stating that the tenant is not allowed to drink excessively in the house is not suitable for conversion into a smart contract. Additionally, storing large-scale data on the blockchain is not feasible as it can increase the cost of running smart contracts on the blockchain, especially on public chains like Ethereum. Therefore, this paper proposes a pattern for assessing the necessity and feasibility of blockchain deployment, referred to as the blockchain pattern. This pattern specifies which content within the clauses of a multi-party service agreement can be converted into smart contracts with controllable costs. It is divided into activity pattern and constraint pattern. Activity pattern refers to a pattern with complete behaviors, which can be expressed as a process. Constraint pattern, on the other hand, refers to the pattern of behavioral execution conditions, usually checking whether a certain condition is met.

To obtain a convincing blockchain pattern, we analyzed 200 textual contracts [17] from various fields using the LLM, summarized and categorized them, resulting in the blockchain pattern presented in Table 1. For this specific blockchain pattern, we conducted blockchain feature extraction, which involves accurately identifying key clauses from the agreement text that comply with the blockchain pattern. Through this process, we screened out a series of clauses that meet the blockchain pattern and used them as outputs. These clauses typically cover the core elements of the agreement, such as the rights and obligations of each party, transaction conditions, and liability for breach of contract. They serve as the core content for blockchain recording and will be permanently stored on the blockchain. Subsequently, we employed advanced LLM to conduct a thorough syntactic dependency analysis of these clauses. This step aims to reveal the dependency relationships between sentence components in the clauses and further extract key sentence components such as subjects, objects, and predicates. Through syntactic dependency analysis, we can gain a clearer understanding of the grammatical structure and semantic information of the clauses, thereby more accurately identifying the elements for blockchain deployment. These blockchain elements will directly influence the modeling process of CIM, as elaborated in Chapter 4.

# 3.3 Modeling of multi-party service agreement based on model driven architecture

MDA is based on a series of open industry standards such as Unified Modeling Language (UML) and Extensible Markup Language (XML), which endows MDA with high compatibility and integrability, enabling seamless integration with various mainstream development tools and platforms. Meanwhile, MDA possesses the capabilities of model visualization, storage, and transformation. This allows developers to intuitively understand and manipulate models, while facilitating the sharing and transmission of model information among different teams or projects. Its objective is to generate source code from abstract models through multiple model transformations across and within different MDA levels [18]. Therefore, the method proposed in this paper adopts MDA to construct a blockchain model for multi-party service agreements.

Based on the design standards of MDA, this paper constructs the CIM, PIM, and PSM metamodels for the multi-party service agreement. Among them, the CIM is assisted by the modeling of the blockchain content derived from the blockchain-enabled clauses output by the blockchain analysis module. The PIM is automatically generated from the CIM according to conversion rules without losing the blockchain semantics. The PSM is expressed using smart contract code and is generated by combining the PIM with a smart contract reuse library. This section focuses on the construction of MDA metamodels. In the next section, the automatic generation process of smart contract code will be described in detail.

The CIM for the multi-party service agreement is defined based on the structure and flow of its blockchain content. It is designed to describe critical information and blockchain-enabled clause requirements of the agreement, and it centers around the blockchain needs of the multi-party service agreement, being agnostic to specific technological platforms. We utilize UML class diagrams to express the CIM metamodel, as shown in Fig. 2. The CIM comprises a process model and a structural model. The process model outlines the interaction flow among participants, expressed through BPMN (Business Process Model and Notation) flowcharts, encompassing user tasks, contract tasks, workflows, gateways, and more. The structural model is constructed from information extracted from the service agreement, including participant identities and roles, agreement clauses, data, constraints, and so on, which we represent using UML object diagrams. Within the CIM, we have provided a more detailed definition of data elements. Data is a crucial component in the model, quantifying the rights and obligations among multiple parties. We categorize data into two types: off-chain uploaded data and on-chain computed and recorded data. Off-chain uploaded data often requires mechanisms such as oracle machines to ensure its authenticity, which is one of our future work items. In this paper, we assume that off-chain uploaded data is authentic and trustworthy. On-chain data is further classified into simple data and composite data. Composite data is derived from the combination of multiple simple data types. For instance, a commodity purchase order is a composite data element, composed of information such as purchase time, commodity name, and quantity. Time data, as a critical element throughout the agreement, is closely related to the lifecycle of the agreement, and we have specifically marked it for emphasis.

The PIM for the multi-party service agreement is defined as the design model for its corresponding smart contract, abstracted from the implementation details of any blockchain platform, ensuring compatibility across various blockchain platforms. We continue to utilize UML class diagrams to represent the PIM metamodel, as shown in Fig. 3. The PIM is designed from the technical perspective of the smart contract itself, with the metamodel encompassing the critical elements required for smart contract code, translating businessrelated information into technical-related information. The conversion process from CIM to PIM is based on rule-based mapping. The structural model generates the fundamental smart contract structure, while the process model generates the various functional functions that the smart contract needs to include. This ensures that the business logic captured in the CIM is accurately

translated into the technical requirements of the smart contract in the PIM.

The PSM for the multi-party service agreement is expressed using Solidity smart contract code on the Ethereum platform. This decision is based on the widespread recognition and application of Solidity within the Ethereum ecosystem, as well as its powerful smart contract development capabilities. Our PSM smart contracts are not manually written from scratch, but are automatically generated based on the tight integration of PIM with a smart contract reuse library. This approach not only improves development efficiency but also ensures code consistency and accuracy. The generation process of PSM smart contracts will be described in detail in the next section. This process covers critical content such as the conversion logic from PIM to Solidity code, the integration method of the reuse library, and the steps involved in automated generation.

The PSM metamodel, as shown in Fig. 4, visually represents the fundamental components of a Solidity smart contract. These elements include data types, functions, events, modifiers, and more, which together form the skeleton of the smart contract, ensuring its integrity and functionality. It is worth mentioning that PSM smart contracts exhibit high flexibility and deployability. They can be deployed and executed on Ethereum or other blockchain platforms that support Solidity, enabling PSM to work across platforms and adapt to diverse business scenarios and requirements.

# 3.4 Smart contract code generation

# 3.4.1 Smart contract reuse library based on blockchain patterns

To transform PIM into PSM, specifically by writing smart contract code in Solidity, it is necessary to maintain a smart contract reuse library. The reuse library employed in this article builds upon our prior work [19], with the introduction of reuse for blockchain patterns. This reuse library adopts a multi-level structure, as depicted in Fig. 5, with the complete contract as the largest reusable unit. The lower levels are organized as blockchain pattern layer, composite function layer, functional function layer, and library function layer. The relationship within this reuse library is one-tomany from top to bottom, meaning that a complete contract is composed of multiple blockchain patterns, a blockchain pattern is composed of multiple composite functions, and a composite function is composed of multiple functions. However, the relationship between the functional function layer and the library function layer is more special. They are not simply a compositional relationship; instead, the functional units stored in the functional function layer may call library functions. For example, when developing an NFT-type smart contract, adopting the ERC721 standard requires calling the OpenZeppelin library [20].

# 3.4.2 Code generation

The generation of Solidity smart contracts is the final step of all preceding work, which involves combining PIM and the smart contract reuse library to produce the smart contract code. After reading the elements from PIM, the first step is to obtain the contract name based on the structural model of the multi-party service agreement and assign a compiler version for the Solidity language. Subsequently, all associated data information for the contract is retrieved, including data names, data types, and data visibility. For privacy protection, any data visibility not specified by the user defaults to private, meaning it is not visible to the outside world. The generation process for functions, constraints, and events follows a similar pattern. Here, we describe the generation process using the most complex element, functions, as an example. Algorithm 1 shows the generation process of smart contracts. First, all blockchain patterns in PIM are traversed until there are no remaining patterns without generated code. The current blockchain pattern that requires code generation is matched with the process template layer in the multi-level reuse library. Matching is based on template tags, and it relies on the smart contract reuse library. If a match is successful, all data associated with the blockchain pattern is traversed, and a semantic match is performed with the data descriptions in the matched process from the reuse library. Since the matched process only contains business logic and not data, the data part needs to be filled in. Once the data match is successful, the data from the pattern is filled into the reusable unit, generating the code corresponding to that pattern.If no match is found at this level, the algorithm proceeds to traverse all functions within the pattern until there are no remaining functions without generated code. The current function is then matched with the functional function layer in the reuse library. If a match is successful, the same process of data matching and filling into the reusable unit is followed, generating the code for that function. The generation of constraints and events follows the same approach as functions. In the end, these elements constitute the initial code of a complete smart contract,

# 4 Case study

In order to fully verify the practicality and accuracy of the blockchain analysis method and smart contract generation method proposed in this article, we decided to conduct an in-depth analysis of a real multi-party service agreement text. In the first stage of the implementation process, the blockchain analysis stage, we specially developed a web tool aimed at automatically processing and analyzing the blockchain elements of the text document. The workflow of this tool is very sophisticated. First, it will split the imported agreement text at the clause level to ensure that each clause is accurately identified and processed independently. Subsequently, the tool will sentence each clause that has been split to further refine the analysis granularity. Finally, through in-depth analysis of the content of the sentences, the tool can accurately identify and extract the key blockchain elements in the clauses. Given that complete agreement texts usually contain a large number of clauses, considering space constraints, it is impossible for us to display all content here. Therefore, we deliberately selected representative authorization clauses for detailed demonstration and analysis. The authorization clauses are not only universal and representative, but also fully demonstrate the effectiveness of our blockchain analysis method. As shown in Fig. 6, we present the blockchain analysis results of the authorization clauses, clearly presenting the extraction of key blockchain elements.

Using the results of on chain analysis as strong support, we constructed structural and process models based on the meta model of CIM. In order to ensure the convenience of the modeling process and the smoothness of subsequent conversion and code generation, we have chosen the PlantUML tool for modeling the structural model. This choice is not accidental. The ease of use, integration, and powerful code generation capabilities of PlantUML tools make it an ideal choice for our modeling work. We constructed a process model to demonstrate the operational process of the simple authorization mode involved in the authorization terms and the interaction relationships between the tasks involved. The process model is shown in Fig 7. It should be emphasized that although we have listed a legitimate and typical process here, the execution of smart contracts on the blockchain is actually triggered by external calls, so the specific execution process is not unique, and the diversity of processes depends on the order and method of caller calls. In the constructed structural model, we clearly express the name, description, clause content, involved data elements of the license authorization multiparty service agreement, as well as the blockchain patterns explicitly stated in the clauses and the various tasks covered by these on chain modes. Fig. 8 shows the CIM structure model, this model explains the static structure of the agreement. Through these models, the parties involved in the agreement and the developers of smart contracts can have a deeper understanding of the inherent mechanism of multi-party service agreements for license authorization, providing a foundation for the subsequent generation of smart contracts.

# 5 Discussion

In this chapter, we will address the three research questions raised in the first chapter using the methods mentioned in this paper and compare them with other related tools. To tackle Challenge 1, we have defined a blockchain pattern for multi-party service agreements and developed a tool based on a large text model to extract blockchain-enabled clauses and perform syntactic dependency analysis on them. This tool can extract the components from the agreement text that need to be converted into smart contracts and deployed on the blockchain, thereby resolving the issue of obtaining necessary blockchain content from multi-party service agreement texts. For Challenge 2, we have defined a multi-party service agreement model based on the blockchain pattern that aligns with a model-driven architecture. This model is assisted by the results of blockchain analysis during its generation and enables model-to-model transformations without losing blockchain semantics. The model clearly presents the structural information of the multiparty service agreement and the execution process of its clauses, reducing communication barriers between developers and business personnel. It visually expresses the blockchain requirements of the multi-party service agreement and serves as a sufficient basis for generating smart contract code. To address Challenge 3, we have designed and maintained a smart contract reuse library based on the blockchain pattern. By matching the PIM with the blockchain patterns in the reuse library, we can automatically generate smart contract code, reducing the development cycle and minimizing the possibility of errors during the smart contract development process.

# 6 Conclusion

To address the challenges of developing multiparty service agreements into smart contracts quickly, accurately, and efficiently, such as the complexity and obscurity of agreement texts and cross-domain collaboration barriers, this paper proposes a smart contract generation method for multi-party service agreements based on blockchain patterns and model-driven approaches. This method can convert complex multi-party service agreements from various industries into complete smart contract code tailored to different domains. Our proposed blockchain analysis method efficiently and accurately parses complex multi-party service agreements, retaining only their critical blockchain content, thus reducing the complexity of the agreement texts. Our model-driven architecture-based multi-party service agreement blockchain model integrates structural and process models, clearly demonstrating the essential elements and execution process of the agreement, thereby reducing cross-domain collaboration barriers. Additionally, we maintain a multi-level reuse library based on blockchain patterns that can generate complete smart contracts when matched with PIMs. We validated our proposed method using a real-world clause case, demonstrating its usability and correctness.
