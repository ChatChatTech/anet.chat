# Edge intelligence in wireless networks

Xiaoxian Yang1  · Li Kuang2

Accepted: 5 December 2024 / Published online: 19 December 2024

© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2024

The development of 5G communication and internet of things (IoT) technology, more and more data are generated by a large number of IoT devices at edge network. If these massive amounts of data are transmitted to remote cloud datacenter, it will not only bring huge transmission pressure to the core network, but also incur long transmission delays. Edge intelligence has attracted great attention from the academic and industrial communities all over the world. It enables to run deep model locally on an IoT device to process data generated by the IoT device itself. It requires high computation capacity and power to train these data and infer model using artifcial intelligence algorithms. Therefore, it needs to take advantage of a wide range of available resources across the hierarchy of end devices, edge nodes, and cloud data centers to optimize performance of training and inferencing a deep neural network model. However, it is currently in its early stage for research on edge AI and needs a special communication for the recent advances of edge AI. Therefore, it is lack of summary and discussion for the technology advancement of edge intelligence. After a rigorous reviewing according to relevance, originality, technical novelties, and presentation quality, in this special issue we selected 6 manuscripts. A summary of these accepted papers is outlined below.

In the frst paper entitled “Enhancing the Security of Edge-AI Runtime Environments: A Fine-tuning Method Based on Large Language Models” by Tang et  al., the authors propose a lightweight joint fne-tuning strategybased on Prefx and LoRA, and fne-tuning ChatGPT and ChatGLM to automatically generate high-quality container escape data samples for enhancing existing detectors. The authors conduct extensive experimental evaluations using three pre-existing SOTA container escape detectors, and the fndings demonstrate that the samples generated through this approach can signifcantly enhance the performance and robustness of the detectors.

In the second paper entitled “Enhancing UAV-Assisted Vehicle Edge Computing Networks through a Digital Twin-Driven Task Ofoading Framework” by Zhang et al., the authors propose a digital twin (DT)-driven task ofoading framework for UAV-assisted VECNs. The aim of the proposed framework is to improve the global performance of VECN task ofoading under limited computational and communication resource constraints. Firstly, the authors construct a decentralized ofoading decision-centralized evaluation task offloading framework for UAV-assisted VECNs based on the asynchronous advantage actor-critic (A3C) algorithm. Secondly, the authors integrate the graph attention networks (GAT) into the framework to incorporate the dynamically changing DT network topology information into the state evaluation of VECNs. By simulating a DTdriven multi-UAV cooperative system and comprehensive evaluation of real-world task request datasets. The framework has a better task throughput rate and stability when performing task ofoading in local resource overload and dynamic edge environment scenarios.

In the third paper entitled “ETS-DDPG: An Energy-Efcient and QoS-Guaranteed Edge Task Scheduling Approach Based on Deep Reinforcement Learning” by Zhao et al., the authors develop an edge task scheduling method by synthesizing a M/G/1/PR queuing model for characterizing the workload distribution and a Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) algorithm for yielding high-quality schedules with low energy cost. The authors conduct extensive numerical analysis as well and show that the proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of average task response time and energy consumption.

In the fourth paper entitled “Data Anonymization Evaluation against Re-identifcation Attacks in Edge Storage” by Chen et al., the authors address the challenges that how to evaluate the data anonymization performance and quality. Specifcally, a decision tree-based solution is proposed that can help to evaluate the quality, performance, and the ability against potential re-identifcation attacks. The research background is that Data anonymization algorithms are widely used in the protection of privacy and sensitive information by de-identify key information. The experimental results demonstrate the proposed solution can provide a clear guide on the evaluation.

In the ffth paper entitled “Context-adaptive and QoSguaranteed Flow Scheduling Optimization in Multipath Multimedia Transmission over MPTCP” by Liu et al., the authors develop a context-adaptive QoS optimization solution designed for Multipath-TCP (MPTCP) control layer, in multimedia transmission environment. To improve the QoS of multimedia transmission, the authors jointly consider path scheduler of MPTCP to reduce congestion based on context metrics and a global QoS guarantee approach adjusting tunable context metrics under the analysis of the relationship between QoS metrics and contexts. Then the authors simulate the shared bottleneck experiment, compare the performance of the algorithm the authors proposed with existing algorithms, and verify the good performance of the developed algorithm in improving the throughput while ensuring the fow fairness. The developed approach is different from traditional congestion control algorithm based on packet loss, and it tells whether the packet loss is due to congestion or other reasons, adjusting the congestion window size more adaptively.

In the sixth paper entitled “SOCA: Secure Ofoading Considering Computational Acceleration for Multi-access Edge Computing” by Yi et al., the authors propose a Secure Ofoading Strategy Considering Computational Acceleration, named SOCA, designed to bolster security while preserving ofoading efciency. Specifcally, the secure ofoading problem is modeled as a multi-objective optimization problem by achieving a composite function of latency mitigation and security metrics as the optimization objective, which is solved by the ChaCha20- based ofoading decision algorithm (ChaCha20-ODA). The algorithm employs the ChaCha20 encryption protocol as its security mechanism. By executing a quarter-round function to generate a keystream, it provides robust protection for data tasks, ensuring that the data remains impervious to malevolent interception by adversaries throughout the transmission process. Furthermore, to improve the computational efciency of task ofoading, the algorithm simultaneously leverages both edge and local computing resources, achieving computational acceleration by optimizing the appropriate ofoad ratio.
